This exploratory analysis took a deliberately messy file of 100,000 student records and turned it into an analysable dataset of 99,953 validated student–term records, without imputing a single value and without deleting any plausible extreme observation. The investigation showed that the data quality problems – text-coded missing values, sentinel numbers, inconsistent spelling, impossible dates and contradictory outcomes – were large enough to hide the true relationships entirely: study hours and SGPA appeared uncorrelated before cleaning (r = –0.001) and clearly related afterwards (r = 0.57). Once cleaned, the data describe students whose academic performance moves with their study effort and engagement, whose placement outcomes occur in the final three semesters and are more frequent among those who completed an internship, and whose stress depends more on commuting time than on where they live. Demographic and background factors, in contrast, are unrelated to results. The analysis also exposed structural weaknesses – repeated student IDs, ceiling effects in marks, unreliable record dates and an apparently simulated structure – and the findings should be read as associations in this particular dataset. The recommended next steps are a unique student–term key, documented units, validated dates and longitudinal follow-up before any causal or predictive modelling is attempted.
